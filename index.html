Developing a HALO (High Acuity, Low Occurrence) Procedures Trolley

1. Project Setup & Initial Analysis

RCEM Guide Tip: A good QIP starts with a 'problem' or issue, not a solution. "Every system is perfectly designed to deliver the results it does." Use the tools below to deeply understand why the current situation exists.

Guide: To reduce the median time taken to gather all necessary equipment for critical procedures (e.g., chest drain, surgical airway, pericardiocentesis) from 15 minutes to under 7 minutes within 4 months, by designing and implementing a standardised, sealed HALO trolley.

Problem Analysis Tools

Guide: Explore root causes.
Manpower: Staff unfamiliar with rare kit; high staff turnover; no designated person to check trolley.
Method: No standard process for gathering kit; items restocked ad-hoc.
Materials: Items missing from various locations; expired stock; multiple brands.
Machine (Environment): Resus bay cluttered; equipment stored in multiple rooms.
Measurement: No data on delays; delays accepted as normal for rare events.

Guide: Map your stakeholders to decide how to engage them.
High Power, High Interest (Manage Closely): ED Clinical Lead, Senior Nursing Staff. Involve in all decisions.
High Power, Low Interest (Keep Satisfied): Hospital Manager, Finance Dept. Provide updates on progress and benefits.
Low Power, High Interest (Keep Informed): Junior Doctors, ODPs. Consult for ideas, provide regular feedback.
Low Power, Low Interest (Monitor): Other departments. Minimal effort required.

2. The PDSA Cycle (Model for Improvement)

Use this iterative cycle to test changes on a small scale. Remember the '1:1:1 principle' for initial tests: 1 provider, 1 patient, 1 encounter.

Plan
Do
Study
Act

Plan: Design your test

Guide: Aim: Rapid access to procedural kit.
Primary Driver 1: Equipment is AVAILABLE.
- Secondary Driver: Robust stocking system -> Intervention: Create a daily checklist.
- Secondary Driver: Clear re-ordering process -> Intervention: Laminated card with product codes.
Primary Driver 2: Equipment is ACCESSIBLE.
- Secondary Driver: Logical layout -> Intervention: Co-design layout with nurses/ODPs.
- Secondary Driver: Clear labelling -> Intervention: Use picture labels on drawers.

Outcome Measure (Voice of the patient/system): Time from decision to perform procedure to first action (e.g., skin prep). Measured via simulation or actual events.

Process Measure (Voice of the system): % of times trolley is correctly stocked at start of shift.

Balancing Measure (Look for unintended consequences): Number of items wasted/discarded; staff satisfaction score with new system.

Do: Carry out the test & log events

Guide: Date: [e.g., 08/07/2025]
Action: Ran a simulation of a surgical airway in Resus Bay 1.
Observations: Initial confusion from staff who missed the brief. ODP noted that the cricothyroidotomy kit was too bulky for the designated drawer.
Problems: One scalpel was missing.
Unexpected events: The anaesthetic trainee from ICU commented on how useful it was.

Study: Observe, analyse, and interpret

Guide: Enter comma-separated values, e.g., 12, 11, 13, 8, 7, 7, 6

Guide: The run chart shows a clear downward TREND (5+ consecutive points decreasing) after introducing the trolley.
A SHIFT (6+ points below the initial median) is evident from week 2 onwards, suggesting a sustainable change.
The initial median was 12 mins, the new median is 6.5 mins.
Qualitative feedback from staff was mostly positive, but highlighted issues with the drawer layout.

Act: Decide on the next steps

Guide (Adapt): Based on feedback, we will swap the airway and line drawers. We will also source a smaller cricothyroidotomy kit. A laminated checklist will be attached to the side of the trolley. We will re-measure the process and balancing measures for 2 more weeks before deciding on a full adoption.

3. Change Management & Staff Engagement

RCEM Guide Tip: Over 70% of changes fail due to lack of staff engagement. Use a structured approach to bring people on the journey with you. Remember 'WIIFM' (What's In It For Me?).

Diffusion of Innovation Strategy

How will you engage the different groups in your department?

Innovators & Early Adopters (2.5% + 13.5%): These are your champions. Involve them in the initial design team. Ask them to help with the 'Do' phase and collect feedback. Their enthusiasm is contagious.

Early & Late Majority (34% + 34%): This is the bulk of your staff. They need to see evidence. Show them the run charts. Highlight the benefits to them (less stress, quicker procedures). Provide simple, clear training sessions.

Laggards (16%): Don't focus your energy on convincing them. Make the new process the standard and the path of least resistance. Ensure the system is robust so it's harder to do it the 'old way'. Provide clear, mandatory guidelines.

Dissemination Plan

Target Audience: e.g., Departmental meeting, regional audit meeting, hospital-wide Patient Safety forum.

Key Message (tailor for each audience): e.g., For clinicians: 'This trolley saves you 5 minutes per procedure.' For managers: 'This project improves efficiency and patient safety, with minimal cost.'

Communication Method: e.g., 15-min presentation, A3 poster for the staff room, article in the hospital newsletter, formal write-up for FRCEM.

- Week 5: PDSA Cycle 1 (design trolley).

- Week 7-8: PDSA Cycle 3 (adapt design).

- Week 9-10: Finalise design, implement fully.
- Week 11-12: Collect post-implementation data, analyse results.

- Disseminate findings, write up project.
